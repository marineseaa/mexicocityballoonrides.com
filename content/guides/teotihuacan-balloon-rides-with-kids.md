Children can fly over Teotihuacán, but the minimum age depends on the operator: 4 at several companies, 5 at Volare, Von Navi and the Vuelo en globo Teotihuacán flights, and 6 at Paseos Olmedo. Some also require a height of 1.10 or 1.20 m. Child fares exist: Volare charges MXN 2,500 for ages 5 to 12 on its shared flight, against MXN 2,900 for adults. The harder part for a child is the morning itself, with a pickup around 4:30 a.m., a field near freezing in winter and a tall basket they stand in for up to an hour. Entry to the archaeological zone afterwards is free for under-13s.

Send the operator each child's age and height before you pay, and get the answer in writing.

## Minimum ages and heights by operator

There is no single rule for the valley. Each operator sets its own, and the same company can state it differently in different places. We Fly is the clearest example: its policies page recommends that children be at least 5 years old and 1.10 m tall, its FAQ says the minimum age is 5, its own sales page says children fly from 4 and at least 1 m tall, and its Viator listing, {{< tour-link tour="247495P2" >}}flight with breakfast at La Gruta{{< /tour-link >}}, says the minimum age is 4. When two sources disagree, plan on the stricter one and ask before you book.

|---|---|---|---|
| {{< tour-link tour="171986P1" >}}Volare{{< /tour-link >}} | 5 and over; the child must stand unassisted | None found | That your child can stand through the flight and see over the basket |
| {{< tour-link tour="247495P2" >}}We Fly{{< /tour-link >}} | 5 recommended (policy and FAQ); 4 on the sales page and Viator listing | 1.10 m recommended; 1 m on the sales page | Which rule applies to your booking |
| Von Navi | 5 and over | 1.20 m | Both age and measured height before paying |
| Flying Pictures | 4 and over, with an adult | None found | Whether the basket suits your child's height |
| Sky Balloons | Child fare for ages 4 to 12 on its Aventura package | Not checked | Whether a 4-year-old is accepted on the day |
| TeoTours | Under-4s restricted | None found | Whether 4- and 5-year-olds are accepted by the balloon company that flies |
| Globos Aerostáticos Teotihuacán | 4 and over, with an adult | 1.20 m | Both age and height |
| {{< tour-link tour="193670P3" >}}Paseos Olmedo{{< /tour-link >}} | Not recommended under 6 | None found | Nothing further; the floor is higher |

Several listings sold on this site use 4. Amigo Tours sets the same pair of rules as Globos Aerostáticos Teotihuacán on its Viator listing, at least 4 years old and taller than 1.20 m, and lists under-4s as not suitable on its GetYourGuide version, {{< tour-link tour="696822" >}}balloon flight with optional pyramids tour{{< /tour-link >}}. {{< tour-link tour="323089P1" >}}Luxury Tours{{< /tour-link >}} says its balloon flight is from 4 years of age, and the reseller Civitatis does not take under-4s either. The two {{< tour-link tour="1522004" >}}Vuelo en globo Teotihuacán listings{{< /tour-link >}} on GetYourGuide mark under-5s as not suitable.

Many of the big operators, including {{< tour-link tour="357659P1" >}}Viaja Teotihuacan{{< /tour-link >}}, {{< tour-link tour="366381P1" >}}Tlatoani{{< /tour-link >}} and {{< tour-link tour="482323P1" >}}Skyteam{{< /tour-link >}}, state no minimum age on their listings at all. That is not the same as having none. For any child near a cut-off, send the booking team the child's age on the flight date and height in centimetres, and ask for a clear yes in writing, including whether the pilot can still refuse on the day. Do not book a non-refundable package on the strength of "4 and up" on one page when another page from the same company says 5.

One more trap: some Viator listings carry generic notes such as "infants are required to sit on an adult's lap" or "infants and small children can ride in a pram or stroller." The Luxury Tours listing has both notes and still says the balloon flight is from 4 years. Do not read them as permission to bring a baby into the basket. Amigo Tours bans strollers on its GetYourGuide listing.

### Why height matters as well as age

The basket sides are high for good reasons, and a short child may see mostly wicker. Passengers also need to follow the landing instructions and keep a steady standing position inside the basket. We Fly's policies prohibit lifting a child or letting a child lean out of the basket during the flight, so an age minimum does not guarantee a view. Ask how high the basket sides are on the balloon you will fly in. If the view would be mostly wicker, consider waiting a year.

### The pyramids ticket for children

The balloon ticket and the archaeological zone are separate. Children under 13 enter the zone free, according to INAH's 2026 bulletin, and adults who are foreign visitors pay MX$210 (Mexican nationals and residents MX$105). If a package bundles admission into one price, do not expect a discount because a child enters free: ask the seller for an itemized quote.

## Shared basket or private family balloon

Neither suits every family. The choice depends on your child's temperament, your group size, the budget and the balloon.

### Shared flight: the cheaper choice

On a shared flight your family stands in a basket with other passengers; how many depends on the balloon and the operator's weight calculations. It suits you when:

- every child clearly meets the operator's rules and can follow instructions
- the children are fine standing close to strangers for a 30 to 60 minute flight
- you are happy with the group's timing for check-in and landing
- saving a few thousand pesos matters more than having the basket to yourselves

Ask whether the whole family will stand in the same compartment and whether each child will have a spot with a view. Four tickets do not guarantee four places side by side.

### Private family balloon: control over the group, not a safety shortcut

Volare sells a [private family flight for groups of 3 to 12](https://volare.com.mx/en/family-private-balloon-flight/), with separate adult and child prices. A private booking means no other paying guests in the basket; the pilot and crew are still in charge. It makes sense when:

- you are celebrating a birthday or another family occasion
- your group is large enough that the price gap over a shared flight is small
- you want a calmer group and freedom over your own photos
- you want one basket confirmed for your family

Private does not mean unrestricted. Children still follow every instruction, the capacity and weight limits still apply, and the pilot decides the flight and the landing. Our guide to [shared vs private balloon rides](/guides/shared-vs-private-teotihuacan-balloon-rides/) compares the two, and the [private balloon rides page](/private-teotihuacan-balloon-rides/) lists baskets reserved for your group.

## Keeping children safe in the basket

A balloon ride is a regulated aviation activity with real risk. There have been serious incidents in the Teotihuacán valley, and a child fare says nothing about an operator's safety record. Ask the same questions you would for an adult flight, about who flies the balloon, its AFAC authorization and its insurance, and more about how children are positioned in the basket and what happens in strong wind or a hard landing. Our [Teotihuacán balloon ride safety guide](/guides/teotihuacan-balloon-ride-safety/) has the incident record and the operator checks.

### Five rules to follow with children

1. Never lift a child for a view. Do not let them sit or stand on the basket edge, lean out or climb. Check the view before booking.
2. Make sure each child can stand unassisted. Passengers stand for the whole flight and take a set landing position. Tell the operator in advance about anything that could make that hard.
3. Follow the pilot, not social media. A photo of a child held up over the rim is not permission. If your child cannot understand or follow the briefing, wait until they can.
4. Warn them about the burner. Tell children the loud roar and the blast of heat are normal. Tie back long hair, secure loose clothing and stand where the crew tells you.
5. Accept a weather cancellation. If the operator decides not to fly, that is a safety decision. Do not press the crew to launch because the children are disappointed.

Operators may also refuse passengers with some heart, spine, mobility or other conditions. If a child has a relevant condition, ask the operator for its exact criteria and talk to the child's doctor.

### Is a child under the minimum age ever allowed?

Do not try to get round an age or height rule. For a child below the operator's minimum, watch the launch from the ground and visit the archaeological zone, if the balloon port allows non-flying visitors, which you should confirm. With younger and older siblings, one adult stays on the ground with the younger ones. Do not assume the operator will look after non-flying children or carry them in the chase vehicle; agree where they will wait and when everyone meets up.

## What is hard for children

For most families the hours around the flight are the hard part, so it helps to know what is coming.

The start is very early. Hotel pickups typically run from about 4:30 to 5:30 a.m., and {{< tour-link tour="394927P1" >}}Explora México{{< /tour-link >}} collects between 4:30 and 4:50. Sky Balloons' Aventura package meets at the Angel of Independence at 4:30 a.m. with check-in at 5:45, and Volare asks flight-only guests to reach its balloon port by 5:45, with boarding from 6:15. If you drive, We Fly suggests leaving Mexico City around 3:30 a.m. for a 5:30 check-in. Children will be woken in the dark and spend an hour in a vehicle before anything happens. If your child usually wakes at 7:30, the wake-up and the wait may be harder than the flight.

The field is cold. At the San Martín de las Pirámides weather station the average night-time low is about 2 °C in December, 1.7 °C in January and 2 °C in February, and frost is possible in winter. From May to September the average low is a milder 8 to 9 °C. You will stand outside through check-in, inflation and the flight, so dress a child in layers, a hat and closed shoes. If your package includes a cave breakfast, bring the layers inside too: Chilango describes La Gruta's cave as cool, at about 6 °C.

The basket is tall, and there is no seat. We Fly notes that very small children may struggle to see over the edge, and it prohibits lifting them. Tlatoani does not recommend the flight for anyone with a problem standing, and you climb into the basket to board. Volare's flight lasts 30 to 50 minutes, and other operators fly for up to an hour.

You cannot bring much. Amigo Tours does not allow backpacks, bags or bulky objects in the basket, and Globos Aerostáticos Teotihuacán has lockers for bags. Plan on carrying snacks and spare layers in pockets instead of a day bag. Selfie sticks are banned too.

The day is long. An all-inclusive package with breakfast and the ruins runs 7 to 9 hours, after a 4:30 a.m. start. The [duration guide](/guides/teotihuacan-balloon-ride-duration/) shows how each option splits the time.

### The night before

- Eat an early dinner and lay out everyone's clothes and shoes.
- Charge phones, confirm the pickup point and save the confirmation offline.
- Pack ID for the adults and the documents the operator asks for the children.
- Check whether pickup is from your hotel or a public meeting point. Volare's transfers collect from selected Mexico City hotels, and Globos Aerostáticos Teotihuacán does not collect from private homes or Airbnbs.
- Pack a familiar snack and water for before or after the flight, if the operator allows it.
- Tell the children the flight might not happen if the weather is poor, and decide whether you have a spare morning to rebook.

Do not plan a long, fixed activity straight after the expected return. Leave a buffer, because an early start, delays and the weather can all push the return later.

## What kids should wear and bring

From November to February expect a cold dawn, warm sun after sunrise, and a lot of walking if you visit the ruins afterwards. Layers work better than dressing for the pickup temperature alone. Our [what to wear guide](/guides/what-to-wear-teotihuacan-balloon-ride/) has the full list.

| Bring or wear | Why |
|---|---|
| Closed shoes that fasten | For climbing in, standing and an uneven landing field |
| Long trousers | Easier than shorts or skirts for climbing into the basket |
| A warm layer that comes off | The wait at dawn is cold, even though the burner warms the basket |
| A fitted hat, long hair tied back | Sun later, and nothing loose near the burner |
| Sun cream for later | The archaeological zone has little shade by late morning |
| Water and a small snack | For the waits on the ground; ask before taking food into the basket |
| The child's ID | Volare accepts a school ID, passport or birth certificate |
| A phone or camera on a strap | Dropped items are gone; check the rules on selfie sticks |

Do not bring a stroller into the basket. If you need one at the archaeological zone, ask where it can wait during the flight and how it travels in the vehicle. Do not promise the children the pyramids straight below: the wind sets the route. Our guide to [whether balloons fly over the pyramids](/guides/do-balloons-fly-over-teotihuacan-pyramids/) explains why.

## The pyramids with children

Children under 13 enter the archaeological zone free, according to INAH's 2026 bulletin. Adults who are foreign visitors pay MX$210, and admission is not included in most balloon packages. The zone opens at 8:00, which fits after landing and breakfast. INAH recommends water, a hat and sunscreen, and the day warms fast: average highs at the San Martín de las Pirámides station run about 24 to 29 °C.

Keep expectations simple for climbers. The Pyramid of the Sun cannot be climbed, and INAH closed the Pyramid of the Moon to climbers "until further notice" in April 2026; we found no reopening announcement. Children see the monuments from the Avenue of the Dead, which is a long walk end to end. After a 4:30 a.m. start, a focused walk of an hour or two, adjusted to energy and heat, usually goes better with young children than trying to see every plaza and museum. Stay on the permitted paths.

## Family-friendly ways to do it

Cut the early start. Sleeping in San Juan Teotihuacán the night before saves the most time: We Fly sells a hotel night with a 15-minute transfer to its field. Pair it with a flight-only option and the morning shrinks to about 3 hours at the balloon port. See our [where to stay page](/where-to-stay-teotihuacan-balloon-ride/) and the [flight-only page](/teotihuacan-balloon-flight-only/).

Keep the logistics in one place. Volare serves its Mexican breakfast at the balloon port after the flight, so there is no extra transfer to a restaurant before the ruins. Its no-transport option counts 3 hours from arrival; [our Volare review](/tours/volare-teotihuacan-balloon-flight-from-mexico-city/) has the details.

Go private on the road. {{< tour-link tour="323089P1" >}}Luxury Tours' private tour with a shared balloon{{< /tour-link >}} collects you from your hotel or house in an SUV or minivan, includes the archaeological zone tickets and a guided visit, and flies children from 4. It cost about $360 per person on dates we checked in late October 2026, and [our Luxury Tours review](/tours/luxury-tours-teotihuacan-private-tour-shared-balloon/) covers it.

Split the party if a child is too young. One adult flies while the other takes the younger child to breakfast and meets up at the zone gates at 8:00.

Ask about child fares. Volare and Sky Balloons publish them on their own sites; most marketplace listings price per person, so ask whether a child pays the adult rate.

## If a child gets scared

Do not treat the flight as something a child must finish because it was expensive. Explain in simple words what will happen: the balloon fills, the burner roars, everyone climbs into the basket and the pilot gives instructions. Tell them the ground will get far away, without promising it will feel like standing on the ground.

If a child is upset at check-in, tell the operator before boarding and ask what it can offer. Refunds and changes may depend on how close you are to departure, so read the terms beforehand. A private basket does not stop a child from feeling afraid. Watching the launch from the ground, if the balloon port allows it, and then walking the pyramids may be a better memory than a flight they did not want.

## If the flight is cancelled for weather

The pilot decides whether it is safe to fly, based on wind, visibility and other conditions. A cancellation after a 4 a.m. wake-up is hard on children, but the pilot's safety decision comes first. Policies differ:

- Volare's family private flight page says weather cancellations can be rescheduled or cancelled with no extra fee.
- We Fly's policies say promotional fares are not refundable, and transport and accommodation are non-refundable.

Before booking, ask whether the flight, breakfast, transport and pyramids tickets are refunded or only rebooked if the pilot cancels. If you fly home the next day, a new date is worth less than a refund. Our [weather and cancellations guide](/guides/teotihuacan-balloon-weather-cancellations/) covers the terms in detail.

## Documents and a family booking checklist

Volare asks for official ID at check-in and accepts a school ID, passport or birth certificate for minors. We Fly lets a parent or guardian identify themselves and sign the register for the child. These rules differ by company. Before you pay, confirm that:

- each child's age and height are accepted, with a written answer for any borderline case
- each child can stand for the whole flight and follow the pilot's instructions
- you know the company that flies the balloon, not only the marketplace
- the company has explained its authorization, passenger insurance and safety rules
- the quote shows adult fares, child fares, the age brackets and any weight charges
- you know whether the basket is shared or private
- hotel pickup or meeting point, and the return, are confirmed
- you know whether breakfast, photos, pyramids entry and a guide are included
- you know which ID each adult and child must bring
- you understand the terms for weather cancellations, illness, no-shows and refunds
- an adult has a ground plan for anyone too young to fly

A written reply from the operator matters most when its web pages disagree.

## Is a family balloon ride worth booking?

For children who are old enough, tall enough, happy with heights and able to follow a pilot's directions, a sunrise flight over Teotihuacán is a day they will remember. Dozens of balloons over the ancient city at dawn are unlike any other day trip from Mexico City.

It is also fine not to fly. A child who will be exhausted by a 4:30 a.m. meeting, anxious in a tall basket or below the height rule will not get much from an expensive booking. Watching the morning balloons and exploring the pyramids is a good day in itself. Send each child's age and height to the operator, compare the child fares and the total for your family, and book once you have a clear yes. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is the minimum age for a Teotihuacán balloon ride?

It depends on the operator. Several take children from 4; Volare, Von Navi and the Vuelo en globo Teotihuacán flights from 5; Paseos Olmedo recommends 6 and up. Some add a height rule of 1.10 or 1.20 m.

### Can a baby or toddler fly in the balloon?

No. Every operator that publishes a rule starts at 4 or older, and you cannot hold a small child in your arms in the basket.

### Can a four-year-old ride?

With some operators, such as Globos Aerostáticos Teotihuacán, Amigo Tours and Flying Pictures, yes, if the child also meets any height rule. Others start at 5, and We Fly's own pages disagree. Get a written yes for your child before booking.

### Is a five-year-old old enough?

Five meets the published minimum at Volare, Von Navi and the operators that start at 4. Height, the ability to stand and follow instructions, and health still decide it.

### Do children pay full price?

Not always. Volare's shared flight costs MXN 2,500 for ages 5 to 12 against MXN 2,900 for adults, and Sky Balloons' Aventura package charges MXN 2,500 for ages 4 to 12. Most marketplace listings price per person, so ask.

### Can my child sit down in the basket?

No. Passengers stand for the flight and take a set landing position. Do not plan on a seat unless the operator confirms one.

### Can I lift my child so they can see the pyramids?

No. We Fly's policies prohibit lifting a child or letting one lean out of the basket. Check before booking whether a shorter child can see over the rim while standing.

### Will the children fly in the same basket as their parents?

Usually, if you book together, but confirm it, especially for larger groups that may need more than one basket. Ask whether you share the compartment with strangers.

### Is a balloon ride suitable for a child afraid of heights?

It depends on the child. The flight is smooth in calm air, but the height, the burner noise and the landing can still upset them. Talk it through beforehand, do not push a reluctant child, and consider watching from the ground.

### Is it too cold for kids?

Winter dawns at the field average about 2 °C and can drop below freezing. Dress children in layers, a hat and gloves. Mornings from May to September are milder.

### Do kids pay to enter the pyramids?

No. INAH lists children under 13 as exempt from the admission fee. That is separate from the balloon fare, so ask how an all-inclusive package handles it.

### Is an all-inclusive family trip better than driving ourselves?

A package takes care of the pre-dawn logistics; driving or a flight-only ticket gives you more flexibility with tired children. Compare the whole day's cost, the pickup point, the return time and the ruins visit, not only the balloon fare.

### What if a child wakes up sick on flight day?

Do not bring an unwell child to save the ticket. Contact the operator straight away and ask what its illness and rebooking terms allow; operators often treat illness differently from a weather cancellation they make themselves.
