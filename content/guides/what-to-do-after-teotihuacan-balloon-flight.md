Walk the pyramids. The archaeological zone opens at 8:00, minutes from the landing fields, and entry costs MX$210 for foreign visitors in 2026, a fee most balloon packages do not include. Add breakfast in a cave restaurant and a short obsidian and pulque workshop, and you are back in Mexico City between about 1:00 and 2:30 p.m. If you have your own transport, the Acolman convent or the Basilica of Guadalupe fit on the way home.

<!--more-->

## The archaeological zone, straight after landing

INAH, the federal institute that runs Teotihuacán, opens the zone Monday to Sunday from 8:00 to 17:00, with last entry at 16:30. The site museums open from 9:00 to 16:30. Balloons take off at first light and fly for 30 to 60 minutes, so after the toast and breakfast you reach the gates in mid-morning with hours to spare.

What you pay at the gate:

- MX$210 for foreign visitors in 2026, about $11. Volare's listing says to bring it in cash.
- MX$105 for Mexican nationals and foreign residents with proof.
- Free for children under 13, people over 60, students and teachers with valid ID, and a few other groups INAH lists. Free Sundays apply only to Mexicans and foreign residents; foreign tourists pay every day.

Tickets are sold at the box offices and through INAH's official site. You enter through one of five gates (Puertas 1 to 5); public buses stop at Puertas 1 and 2, and La Gruta is reached from Puerta 5, behind the Pyramid of the Sun.

What you can and cannot do. Climbing the Pyramid of the Sun is prohibited and has been since 2020; INAH's March 2026 bulletin still lists it as banned. The Pyramid of the Moon reopened for climbing to its first tier in May 2025, but after the shooting there on 20 April 2026 INAH said access stays closed until further notice, and we found no reopening announcement. Plan to walk the Avenue of the Dead, which runs a couple of kilometres end to end, and see the pyramids from the ground. INAH launched a digital guide in June 2026 with seven themed routes in English and Spanish, each with a map, timings and an access gate.

Most packages give you 1 to 1.5 hours of free time on site, which covers the Avenue of the Dead and the base of both pyramids at a steady pace. Some flights include the entry ticket, and a few add a guide: our [balloon and pyramids tour page](/teotihuacan-balloon-and-pyramids-tour/) compares them, and our guide to [operator-direct combo prices](/guides/teotihuacan-balloon-ride-pyramids-tour-packages/) checks each package's inclusions.

## Breakfast in the cave: La Gruta and the alternatives

La Gruta is a restaurant inside a natural volcanic cave, about 200 m from Puerta 5, outside the pyramid core. It dates from the early 20th century (sources disagree on the year), seats around 700 according to Atlas Obscura, and its website lists it as open daily from 8:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. Reservations are advised at weekends, and the cave is cool inside, so keep a layer handy.

Several balloon packages build the morning around it. We Fly sells a flight with breakfast at La Gruta, and warns on its listing that the wait outside the restaurant is beyond its control. Paseos Olmedo's {{< tour-link tour="193670P3" >}}balloon flight with a stop at La Gruta{{< /tour-link >}} includes the stop but not the food. Many other operators describe their breakfast as "in a natural cave" without naming the venue, and some use local restaurants instead: La Higuera, opposite the pyramids, on Globo Tetris's all-inclusive option, and Huehueteotl on one Explora México option.

If your package has no breakfast, the cave makes a good stop on foot between the landing and the gates. Our page on [balloon rides with cave breakfast](/teotihuacan-balloon-rides-with-breakfast/) lists which packages include it and what they cost, and our guide to [La Gruta vs La Cueva](/guides/teotihuacan-balloon-ride-cave-breakfast/) explains the two cave restaurants.

## Obsidian, maguey and pulque workshops

The third stop on most full-day packages is an artisans' cooperative near the site. The format is similar everywhere: a short talk on obsidian, the black volcanic glass the Teotihuacanos worked, and on the maguey plant, followed by a tasting of local drinks and time in the shop.

What the listings promise:

- Viaja Teotihuacan: a cultural explanation of obsidian, quartz and maguey, with a liquor tasting, on its full option.
- Skyteam Teotihuacán: obsidian and maguey, pigment techniques and a tasting of local drinks.
- Luxury Tours: pulque, tequila and mezcal, then an obsidian craft house.
- Amigo Tours LATAM: 45 minutes at Artesanías El Quetzal, with a tequila tasting.
- A Civitatis balloon tour describes obsidian, agave and aguamiel fermentation and silver work, with pulque, tequila, nopal liqueur and mezcal to taste.

It is a pleasant half hour, and the tasting is part of the price. If you are short on time or want longer at the ruins, ask whether your group can skip it; on flight-only bookings you can simply leave.

## Acolman: the convent on the Mexico City side

The former Augustinian convent of San Agustín Acolman, now INAH's Museo Virreinal, sits roughly 10 km southwest of the pyramids in a straight line, on the Mexico City side of the valley. INAH lists it as open Monday to Sunday from 9:00 to 17:30. Its 2022 guide gave the entry fee as MX$65, which may have changed; Sundays are free for Mexican visitors, and under-13s, students, teachers and seniors also enter free.

Acolman suits two kinds of traveller: those who drive themselves to a [flight-only booking](/teotihuacan-balloon-flight-only/), and those on a private tour who can ask the driver for a detour. None of the balloon listings in our data include it in the itinerary.

## The Basilica of Guadalupe on the way back

The Basilica of Guadalupe stands in the north of Mexico City, about 37 km southwest of the site and close to Insurgentes Norte, the road most tours use to leave and re-enter the city. One news outlet gives its hours as 6:00 to 20:00 daily; confirm on the basilica's own site before you go.

The easiest way to add it is to book it with the flight. ## Realistic return times

Your return time depends on what the package includes, not on how long the flight lasts. What operators say:

| Day you booked | Back in Mexico City |
|---|---|
| Flight with immediate return (GA-MX) | About 10:00 |
| Flight, La Gruta stop and free time (Paseos Olmedo) | Tour ends about 12:00 |
| Private transport and 2.5-hour guided site visit (Excellentravel) | About 1:00 p.m. |
| Flight, cave breakfast, tasting and 1 hour at the ruins (Explora México) | About 1:00 to 2:00 p.m. |
| Flight plus Teotihuacán tour (GA-MX) | About 2:30 p.m. |
| Full day with the Basilica (Amigo Tours LATAM) | Afternoon; listed duration up to 12 hours |

The drive back is roughly 45 to 60 minutes in normal traffic, longer at midday on a weekday. After a 4:30 a.m. pickup most people are tired by early afternoon, so an evening plan in the city is realistic, a second big sight is not. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is entry to the pyramids included in a balloon ride?

Usually not. Most packages exclude the MX$210 site fee, and some list it as included on their top option only. Check the "includes" line of your exact option before you book.

### Can you climb the pyramids at Teotihuacán in 2026?

The Pyramid of the Sun cannot be climbed; INAH still prohibits it. The Pyramid of the Moon's first tier reopened in May 2025 but closed again after 20 April 2026 "until further notice", and we found no reopening.

### How much time do you get at the ruins after the flight?

Most all-inclusive packages give 1 to 1.5 hours of free time. Excellentravel's private-transport option includes a guided visit of about 2.5 hours, and We Fly's all-inclusive option on one listing gives 3 hours.

### Can I visit the Basilica of Guadalupe on the same day?

Yes. It is on the north side of Mexico City, near the road back from Teotihuacán, and Amigo Tours LATAM sells a balloon option with a guided Basilica visit. With a flight-only booking and your own car, it is an easy stop on the drive home.

### What time do you get back to Mexico City?

Around 10:00 if you return straight after the flight, about 1:00 to 2:30 p.m. with breakfast and the ruins, and later on full-day tours that add the Basilica.
