A Teotihuacán balloon ride is a regulated commercial flight, and it is not risk-free. AFAC, Mexico's federal civil aviation agency, requires a permit, insurance and licensed crews, and in 2026 it stepped up checks in the valley after a run of incidents. No public list of authorized operators exists, so the useful checks are yours: find out which company actually flies the balloon, ask for its AFAC permit, the pilot's licence and proof of passenger insurance, read its weather and refund terms, and listen to the briefing. Health and age rules differ by operator and are strict, so read your listing's restrictions before you pay.

## Who regulates the flights, and what changed in 2026

AFAC (Agencia Federal de Aviación Civil), part of Mexico's transport ministry, SICT, is the authority for commercial balloon flights. After the 2023 fire, SICT's communiqué of 2 April 2023 set out what a balloon company needs: an AFAC permit, compliance with the official standard NOM-008-SCT3-2002, valid insurance covering passengers and third parties, a crew holding the relevant aeronautical licence and qualification, and proper maintenance of the aircraft. In March 2026 AFAC restated the rules: companies must meet operation, maintenance and training requirements, including periodic inspections, pilot certification, emergency protocols and suitable weather.

The bigger change came on the ground. In February 2026 the authorities announced a permanent AFAC operational commission for the valley, with a daily presence on site, working with civil protection and the municipal offices of San Juan Teotihuacán and San Martín de las Pirámides. Its job, as announced, is to check the condition of balloons and facilities, airworthiness certificates and landing procedures, and to decide whether the weather allows flights. Milenio reported on 30 April 2026 that the commission was operating from 5:00 to 9:00 a.m. with AFAC present, that a pilot review programme had started on 25 April, and that 24 companies offered balloon rides in the two municipalities. Two companies from outside the area, one from Atizapán and one from Pachuca, were flagged as operating in the airspace.

The scale matters when you read the incident list below. The 2026 reports describe more than 20 companies and, on one inspection day, 46 balloons in operation. That is a busy sky. Whether the new commission is still running every morning in November 2026 is not something we could confirm from the reports.

A website that says "certified", "insured" or "licensed" is making a claim. It is not proof. Ask for the legal name of the business that operates the balloon, the type and current status of its authorization, and how the passenger insurance applies to your booking. You do not need to read the technical rules yourself, but a good company answers these questions plainly.

## The March 2026 inspection and the three suspensions

On 29 March 2026 AFAC carried out a verification visit to the balloon ports of the Teotihuacán valley with state and municipal authorities, civil protection and the state's verification institute. In its communiqué of 3 April 2026, AFAC said inspectors checked 23 of the 46 balloons in operation that day, and owners showed their documentation.

Three businesses were suspended and sealed by civil protection: Volar en Globo, Sky Ballons and Nuevo Ciclo. The communiqué gave the reason only as "various irregularities detected during the inspection". It did not say what the irregularities were, so it is not known whether they were paperwork problems or anything that affected passengers. We could not find whether the three businesses have since reopened, so do not assume either way: ask any of them directly. The inspection also covered only half of the balloons in operation that day, so the other 23 were not checked on that visit, and the result says nothing either way about them.

What a traveler can take from this: inspections happen, they find problems, and the names involved were made public. If you book through a reseller, find out which balloon company actually flies you, and compare that exact name, not a similar-sounding brand. Several listings send the exact balloon-port location only in a message the day before, so ask earlier if you want to know.

### Does a fatal accident mean you should never fly?

It means you should take the activity seriously. People decide differently about an optional flight, especially with children or a relative with a medical condition. If you want a low-uncertainty day, you can see the pyramids from the ground. If you are comfortable with the remaining risk, insist on clear documentation and a professionally run operation.

Be wary of any article that gives a precise "one in X" risk for Teotihuacán: no public dataset of flights and incidents supports one. Be wary too of a company calling itself "the safest operator" without independent evidence.

## How to choose a Teotihuacán balloon operator: 10 checks

Use these checks whether you book with an operator directly, on a tour platform, over WhatsApp or through a hotel concierge. Authorities quoted by Posta in February 2026, and Chilango in 2023, advise confirming that a company holds a valid AFAC permit before you board, and asking for proof of insurance and the pilot's licence.

### 1. Find out who operates the balloon

A marketplace, travel agency or day-trip organizer may sell a package without owning or flying the balloon. The balloon company may differ from the one driving you from Mexico City or guiding you around the pyramids. Ask: "What is the legal name of the company that will fly the balloon, and where is its balloon port?" If the seller gives only the marketplace name, or will not say until the morning of the flight, ask again before you commit.

### 2. Ask about the AFAC permit

Ask the company, before booking, which current authorization it holds, and get enough detail to check it through official channels if you need to. A reseller should be able to tell you which company flies. A screenshot of a permit without context may be out of date or belong to another company. A warning sign: "All the balloons here are authorized", with no detail about this operation.

### 3. Confirm the pilot's licence

A commercial tourist balloon needs a licensed pilot. Ask at booking whether the assigned pilot holds a current licence, and ask again at check-in: it is a reasonable question and a good company answers it. A social-media claim of thousands of flying hours is not a licence. Many operators state their credentials in their listings. {{< tour-link tour="456146P3" >}}Visita Teotihuacán{{< /tour-link >}}, for example, says it has all the documentation and certifications AFAC requires, and {{< tour-link tour="247495P2" >}}We Fly{{< /tour-link >}} says its pilots are AFAC-certified. These are the companies' own statements, which is why asking costs nothing.

### 4. Ask about maintenance and the aircraft

You cannot assess balloon fabric, burners or fuel systems yourself. You can ask whether the balloon has its required documents and a maintenance programme, and which aircraft will carry you. A specific answer tells you more than "Don't worry, our balloons are new". A new balloon can still be badly operated, and an older one can be well maintained.

### 5. Confirm the passenger insurance

Some listings name it. {{< tour-link tour="247495P2" >}}We Fly's flight with breakfast at La Gruta{{< /tour-link >}} lists travel insurance among its inclusions, and {{< tour-link tour="487546P4" >}}Vive Vuela{{< /tour-link >}} and {{< tour-link tour="484176P1" >}}Arc Tours{{< /tour-link >}} list passenger insurance during the flight. We Fly asks for photo ID at check-in because guests are registered under the flight insurance. Ask what the cover includes, who the insurer is and whether it applies to every passenger on your booking. Insurance protects you financially; it does not prove that an operation is safe, and "insurance included" on a listing tells you nothing about limits or exclusions.

### 6. Read the weather-cancellation policy

The operator should tell you who makes the go or no-go decision, when you hear about it, and what happens to your payment if weather stops the flight. Read these separately:

- flights the operator cancels for unsafe conditions
- cancellations and date changes you ask for
- transport, meals and hotel nights, whether included or booked separately
- refund versus credit or a new date

These are not the same thing. We Fly, for example, does not refund promotional fares, and its transport and accommodation are non-refundable. "Free cancellation" on a booking platform may not cover every part of a package. Our [guide to booking a Teotihuacán balloon ride](/guides/how-to-book-teotihuacan-balloon-ride/) compares the terms.

### 7. Check the basket capacity and what "private" means

A shared balloon can be entirely legitimate, and a private basket is not safer in itself. What matters is that the load stays within the aircraft's limits and that every passenger gets instructions and room to stand. Ask how many passengers the basket carries, what "private" means on this booking, and the weight rules. A booking "for two" does not mean only two people on board; our [balloon ride for two guide](/guides/teotihuacan-balloon-ride-for-two/) explains the difference between two seats on a shared flight and a private basket.

### 8. Check age, health and mobility rules before paying

Rules differ by operator; the table further down sets them out. Minimum ages run from 4 to 6 depending on the company, and pregnancy, recent surgery, heart conditions, spine, hip or knee problems, or difficulty taking the landing position can rule you out. Your details matter too: by government order, according to Amigo Tours' Viator listing, operators must collect each passenger's name, weight and health conditions. Give your true weight; it is used to load the basket. If you have a health condition or limited mobility, ask the operator in advance and get medical advice if you need it.

### 9. Check the ground operation, not just the flight

A full day can include a pre-dawn pickup in Mexico City, a roadside meeting point, several transfers, a launch field, a chase vehicle and a landing in an open field. Ask who provides each part and how you are collected after landing. Will a driver or guide stay in contact? Is the recovery vehicle part of the package? Does the return have a fixed time? What happens if the balloon lands far from the launch field?

### 10. Look for clear answers, not a perfect rating

An operator that patiently explains its restrictions, documents, decisions and cancellation terms gives you more to go on than an anonymous five-star review. Read recent reviews for patterns in communication, safety briefings, punctuality and how the company handled bad weather. Reviews can show patterns; they cannot show regulatory compliance.

## A safety message you can send the operator

You can send this before booking. It is polite and specific:

> Hello, I'm considering a Teotihuacán hot air balloon ride on [date]. Could you please confirm the legal name of the company that operates the balloon, its current AFAC authorization, the pilot's qualifications, and what passenger insurance is included? I would also like to know the passenger and weight restrictions, how the flight is cancelled for unsafe weather, and what refund or rescheduling terms apply to the balloon and the transport. A good reply answers each point, or names the person who can, without pushing you to pay at once.

## Weather, sunrise and the pilot's call

Weather matters more than anything else for whether a balloon flies. A balloon depends on wind speed and direction, visibility, rain, the terrain and the pilot's reading of conditions at take-off, in flight and at landing. A forecast for central Mexico City cannot tell you whether a balloon can fly over Teotihuacán at dawn.

Flights launch early. Volare's published schedule has passengers at the balloon port at 5:45 a.m., boarding from 6:15 and flying from about 6:30 to 7:10, with a flight of 30 to 50 minutes depending on the weather. We Fly's policies list heavy rain and winds over 15 km/h as grounds for cancellation, and its FAQ adds dense fog. That is one company's stated threshold and does not apply across the valley; other aircraft and operators decide differently, and no single wind figure tells you whether a flight should go.

Globos Aerostáticos Teotihuacán states that the pilot decides whether take-off happens, and that an early landing for safety is not grounds for a refund. A cancelled or shortened flight is the system working. Our [weather and cancellations guide](/guides/teotihuacan-balloon-weather-cancellations/) covers the months and the refund terms.

### Is a cancelled balloon ride a bad sign?

Not on its own. Cancelling for unsuitable weather fits a careful operation. What matters is whether the operator explains the decision, honours its published terms and tells you your options. Be more concerned if staff wave away worsening conditions, discourage questions, or promise a particular route and sunrise view whatever the wind.

### Is sunrise always the safest time?

Pilots fly at sunrise because the early air is often calmer, but sunrise guarantees nothing. Fog, changing winds and storms can still stop an early launch, and the pilot has to judge the conditions on the day. Our [Teotihuacán sunrise balloon guide](/guides/teotihuacan-sunrise-hot-air-balloon-ride/) covers launch times and what to expect.

## What happens at the balloon port

A well-run morning is reassuring, but it does not replace the paperwork. These steps should be visible:

1. Check-in: the company confirms who is flying and any restrictions. Bring the ID your confirmation asks for.
2. Crew preparation: the crew prepares the balloon while passengers stay where they are told, clear of burners, fuel, vehicles and ropes.
3. Safety briefing: before take-off, listen for how to climb in and out, where to hold on, how to behave in flight and the landing position. A short briefing is listed by several operators, and several listings name a safety briefing or insurance among their inclusions. Ask if anything is unclear, or not in a language you understand.
4. In flight: keep your body inside the basket and follow the pilot's directions about moving and taking photos. Do not touch the controls or equipment.
5. Landing and recovery: be ready for a firm landing and stay in the basket until the pilot tells you to climb out. The balloon often lands away from the launch field, and the chase crew collects you.

These are general expectations; on the day, follow the briefing for your aircraft and conditions.

### What if there is no safety briefing?

Ask for one before boarding, and ask for it in a language you understand. If the crew refuses or brushes off a reasonable concern, you do not have to board. Note what happened and take it up with the company that sold the booking.

## Where the risks sit, stage by stage

Each part of the morning carries its own concerns. They are not equally likely, and no reliable local risk rate exists.

| Stage | Possible concern | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| Inflation and boarding | Hot burners, moving equipment and vehicles | Stay in the area you are given and follow the crew. |
| Take-off | The basket moves and your footing shifts | Hold the points you are shown and stand where the pilot puts you. |
| Flight | Changing winds, height, other balloons and obstacles | Stay inside the basket and leave the equipment alone. |
| Approach and landing | Uneven ground, a firm touchdown, the basket tipping, power lines | Take the exact landing position the pilot describes and do not climb out early. |
| Ground transfer | Early starts, traffic, confusion over meeting points | Get the driver, vehicle and meeting point in writing. |
| Weather cancellation | Losing prepaid parts of the day | Read the flight and transport cancellation terms before you pay. |

The record includes a fire, a power-line strike and landings away from the planned field. Take the safety process seriously for that reason; photographs and general reviews cannot tell you how your own morning will go.

## Private or shared: is one safer?

Not necessarily. A private basket gives your group the passenger space to yourselves, as defined by the booking. It does not change the weather, remove landing hazards, prove the pilot's licence or exempt the balloon from maintenance. A well-run shared flight is a better choice than a poorly documented private one, so compare the operator, its procedures and its paperwork first, and privacy, basket size and price second. ### Can children, pregnant travelers or people with health conditions fly?

Do not assume everyone in your party can. Check the operator's rules and each person's circumstances:

- Children: minimum age, height and whether the child can follow the landing instructions. Each operator sets its own minimum age.
- Pregnancy: excluded or not recommended by every operator in the table above.
- Recent surgery, fractures or joint problems: these can make it unsafe to climb in, stand for the flight or take the landing position.
- Heart, lung and other serious conditions: get medical advice and confirm with the operator before booking.
- Limited mobility: standard baskets can be hard or impossible to climb into and out of, and an adapted arrangement cannot be assumed.
- Alcohol or drugs: operators bar passengers who are under the influence.

On a family booking, confirm the rules for each child. A child old enough for a ticket may still not be able to follow the briefing, and an adult must stay with them in the basket.

## Two kinds of insurance to check

Two kinds of cover matter, and they are separate:

1. The operator's passenger and liability insurance: ask for the details of the policy on your flight, who it covers and its limits and exclusions.
2. Your own travel insurance: check whether it covers hot air ballooning, medical care, transport and changes to the rest of the trip if the flight is cancelled or you are hurt.

"Insured" on a tour listing does not mean that your flight, belongings, non-refundable hotel and every medical cost are covered. The UK government's [Mexico health advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/mexico/health) recommends checking what healthcare and insurance you may need before you travel.

## When to choose not to board

You do not need to argue aviation rules on the launch field. You can simply decide not to fly if:

- nobody will tell you which company or pilot is responsible for the flight
- the crew will not give a briefing you can understand
- a medical or mobility issue you declared was ignored, or staff push you to set aside a restriction
- you are told to do something that contradicts the briefing and get no clear explanation
- you are pressed to fly despite concerns about the conditions, without a clear decision from the crew

Having paid is not a reason to ignore your concerns. If you decide not to board, note the company, date, booking details and what happened, then contact the seller about its terms. Declining to board does not automatically entitle you to a refund.

## If the flight is cancelled or something goes wrong

If the flight is cancelled before take-off, get the reason in writing and ask whether you are offered a refund, a credit or a new date. Breakfast, a guide or transport you have already received may be treated separately from the flight.

If you are delayed after landing, stay with the crew and call the tour's operations or emergency number instead of the sales line. Do not set off on foot across unfamiliar fields or roads unless you are directed to a safe meeting place.

If someone is hurt, follow the crew and the emergency services. Mexico's emergency number is 911. Contact your travel insurer and, if needed, your embassy or consulate, and keep receipts and notes for any later claim. Visit México's [emergency numbers page](https://visitmexico.com/en/pagina/emergencias) and the UK government's [getting help in Mexico](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/mexico/getting-help) page list the contacts.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a Teotihuacán hot air balloon ride safe in 2026?

Commercial flights are legal and regulated by AFAC, but no flight is risk-free. After the 2026 incidents and suspensions, confirm the individual operator's authorization, the pilot's licence, the passenger insurance and the weather policy. No independent safety ranking of the current operators exists.

### Who regulates hot air balloons at Teotihuacán?

AFAC, the federal civil aviation agency. Operators need an AFAC permit, valid insurance and licensed staff, and since 2026 a permanent AFAC commission has been announced for daily checks in the valley.

### Has there been a fatal balloon accident at Teotihuacán?

Yes. On 1 April 2023 a balloon caught fire in flight and fell; two passengers died, and press reports said a 13-year-old girl was injured. We found no fatal balloon fire in 2024 or 2026.

### Which companies did AFAC suspend in 2026?

After the 29 March 2026 inspection, Volar en Globo, Sky Ballons and Nuevo Ciclo were suspended for irregularities that AFAC did not specify. We could not confirm whether they have reopened.

### How can I check a balloon company before booking?

Ask for its legal name, its AFAC permit, the pilot's licence and proof of passenger insurance. There is no public AFAC list of authorized Teotihuacán operators that we could find, so you have to ask.

### Which Teotihuacán balloon company is the safest?

No public data supports naming one. Compare each company's credentials, current regulatory position, weather procedures, communication and insurance. A marketing ranking or a star rating will not tell you.

### Is a private balloon ride safer than a shared one?

Not automatically. A private basket changes who stands with you. Safety still depends on the aircraft, crew, conditions and operating practice.

### What if it is windy on the morning of my flight?

The crew decides whether a safe launch and flight are possible. Expect a delay, a shorter flight or a cancellation, and do not treat a general forecast or one wind figure as permission to fly.

### Can a balloon land somewhere other than the launch field?

Yes. The wind sets the route, so balloons often land away from the launch field and a recovery vehicle collects you. Confirm the pickup is included and follow the pilot's landing instructions.

### Can I fly if I am pregnant or have a heart condition?

No. Pregnancy is excluded or not recommended by every operator in the table above, and most of them also rule out heart or cardiovascular problems.

### Can I get a refund if I feel unsafe and decline to board?

There is no general answer. It depends on who sold the booking, the contract terms and the facts. Keep notes and contact the seller.

### Is the balloon flight part of the Teotihuacán archaeological site visit?

No. The balloon launches from a balloon port in the wider valley, and admission to the archaeological zone is a separate ticket unless your package says it is included.
