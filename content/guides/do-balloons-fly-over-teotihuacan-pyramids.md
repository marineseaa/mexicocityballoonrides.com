Sometimes. Balloons launch from fields in the valley around the archaeological zone, and on some mornings the wind carries them over or right beside the Pyramid of the Sun, the Pyramid of the Moon and the Avenue of the Dead. On others it carries them across the valley with the pyramids in view but at a distance. No operator can promise which, and the honest listings say so in their small print.

<!--more-->

## Why the wind decides the route

A hot air balloon has no engine pushing it forward and no rudder. Explora México spells it out on its listing: the pilot only controls the ascent and descent. By climbing or dropping, a pilot can look for air moving in a different direction, and that is what operators mean when they say they "seek currents" toward the archaeological zone. We Fly says this on its own website, and adds that reaching the zone depends on the day's wind and weather.

The rest of the small print agrees. Volare's listing says the direction and speed of the flight depend on the wind, "so sometimes there is the possibility of not flying over the archaeological site." The Vuelo en globo Teotihuacán listings on GetYourGuide say the views of the pyramids depend on the weather and the direction of the wind. Globos Aerostáticos Teotihuacán goes furthest: its terms say it does not guarantee that the aircraft follows a direction or overflies a specific route. The reseller Civitatis puts it in five words: flying over the ruins isn't guaranteed.

Flights only run in light winds. Press reports quoting balloon experts put the usual limit at around 10 to 15 km/h, while Globos Aerostáticos Teotihuacán cancels above 20 km/h. Within that limit, the direction of the breeze on the morning decides whether the drift runs toward the zone or away from it. Neither you nor the pilot chooses the answer the evening before, and a flight that misses the pyramids is not grounds for a refund under any policy we have seen.

## What you can realistically see

Most launch fields sit in San Martín de las Pirámides and the farmland around the zone, so the pyramids are part of the view from the moment you lift off. The site runs a couple of kilometers end to end along the Avenue of the Dead, so even a flight that drifts away from it usually keeps the pyramids on the horizon. One of the Vuelo en globo Teotihuacán listings describes exactly that outcome, the pyramids "in the distance" as the sunrise lights the sky.

When the drift runs toward the zone, the monuments to look for are the Pyramid of the Sun, the Pyramid of the Moon, the long straight line of the avenue itself, and the Temple of Quetzalcoatl. Civitatis lists those four on its balloon itinerary. We Fly's FAQ describes its flights drifting over the Sun, the Moon and the Avenue of the Dead "when conditions allow."

There is evidence that balloons do pass over the site. In April 2026 a balloon made an emergency landing next to the Pyramid of the Sun, on the Avenue of the Dead, according to reports citing AFAC. It shows the zone lies inside the area where balloons fly; it is not a sign that every flight crosses it.

The other thing you will see is other balloons. Reports from the March 2026 AFAC inspection counted 46 balloons in operation across the valley, so expect plenty of them in your photos. Sunrise at Teotihuacán falls around 6:33 in late October and 7:05 in late December, so most flights are in the air for the first warm light on the pyramids. Our [photos guide](/guides/teotihuacan-balloon-ride-photos/) covers how to make the most of it.

## Is it allowed to fly over the pyramids?

We found no published rule either way. No INAH regulation, AFAC exclusion zone or minimum altitude specific to overflying the archaeological zone appears in any source we checked, and press coverage of the 2026 investigations notes that the official reports say nothing on whether overflight is permitted or regulated. That means two things for you. Do not believe a seller who tells you overflight is banned as a reason the flight went elsewhere, and do not believe one who promises a set height over the Pyramid of the Sun. Neither claim has a public source.

## Questions to ask your operator

You cannot choose the wind, but you can find out how an operator handles it:

- Which direction have flights been drifting this week, and how often have they passed over the zone?
- Where is your launch field relative to the archaeological site? We Fly puts its reception 5 minutes from the zone; other operators are further out in San Martín de las Pirámides.
- Does the pilot try to steer toward the pyramids by changing height, and how long is the flight if the wind is light?
- If the wind is too strong to fly, what happens to my booking? Our [weather and cancellations guide](/guides/teotihuacan-balloon-weather-cancellations/) sets out each operator's policy.

Season helps with the sky, not the direction. We Fly says the dry season, roughly November to April, brings the clearest skies and the most reliable launches. The [best time guide](/guides/best-time-teotihuacan-balloon-ride/) goes month by month.

## Seeing the pyramids for certain

If walking among the pyramids matters as much as the flight, book a package with time at the ruins. The archaeological zone opens at 8:00, so it fits neatly after landing and breakfast. Admission was MX$210 for foreign visitors in 2026 and is not included in most balloon packages, while children under 13 and people over 60 enter free. You cannot climb the Pyramid of the Sun; INAH's 2026 bulletin still prohibits it.

Our [balloon and pyramids tour page](/teotihuacan-balloon-and-pyramids-tour/) compares the packages that bundle a guided visit, and [what to do after the flight](/guides/what-to-do-after-teotihuacan-balloon-flight/) covers the ruins on your own.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will my balloon fly directly over the Pyramid of the Sun?

Only if the wind carries it there. Operators look for currents toward the zone, but none guarantees the route, and some flights pass beside the pyramids or at a distance.

### Can I see the pyramids even if we do not fly over them?

Usually yes, because the launch fields are in the valley around the zone. One listing describes the pyramids appearing "in the distance" on flights that drift away.

### Is flying over Teotihuacán illegal?

We found no published rule that bans overflight or sets a minimum altitude over the zone. Treat any claim either way as unsourced.

### Do private balloons get closer to the pyramids?

A private basket changes who flies with you, not the wind. The same drift applies; see our [shared vs private guide](/guides/shared-vs-private-teotihuacan-balloon-rides/).

### Can I get a refund if the balloon misses the pyramids?

No policy we have seen offers one. Refunds and rebooking apply when weather stops the flight, not when the route differs.
