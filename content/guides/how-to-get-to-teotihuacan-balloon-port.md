Teotihuacán's balloon ports are launch fields in the valley around the archaeological zone, mostly in San Martín de las Pirámides, roughly 45 to 50 km by road northeast of Mexico City. You have three realistic ways to be there for a check-in between about 5:30 and 6:00 a.m.: a flight with the operator's Mexico City transfer, a private driver or your own car with the operator's exact pin, or a night in San Juan Teotihuacán before the flight. Public buses do not run early enough, and your operator sends the exact location the day before. Our [day-trip itinerary](/guides/teotihuacan-balloon-ride-from-mexico-city-day-trip-itinerary/) shows how each choice fits into the rest of the morning.

## Five ways to get there, compared

| Transport option | Works for a pre-dawn check-in? | Main advantage | Main drawback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight package with round-trip Mexico City transfer | Yes, when it is arranged for your flight | The driver knows your operator's reception | Fixed pickup and return times; usually costs more than flight-only |
| Private driver or taxi booked in advance | Yes, if confirmed the day before | You choose when to leave and come back | You must give the field's pin and agree the return |
| Your own or a rental car | Yes | You can stay at the pyramids as long as you like | A dark, early drive, plus navigation and parking |
| Uber or DiDi called on the morning | Possible, but risky as your only plan | Convenient if a driver accepts | Availability at 4 a.m. and the ride back are uncertain |
| Public bus from Terminal Norte | No, it starts too late for a sunrise flight | Low-cost way to visit the ruins later in the day | First tickets go on sale at 6:00 a.m., after check-in |

Book transport with your flight if you want the simplest morning. If you would rather buy a cheaper flight-only ticket, arrange a driver for both directions before you pay. Keep the bus for a separate daytime visit to the ruins, or for the ride back to the city after a flight.

## Where the balloon ports actually are

The balloons do not take off from the pyramids, and they do not take off from Mexico City. The launch fields, which operators call "globopuertos" or balloon ports, are spread across the farmland around the zone in the State of Mexico, many of them along the México–Tulancingo road near San Martín de las Pirámides, San Francisco Mazapa and San Juan Teotihuacán. A state official quoted in February 2026 said about 90% of balloons take off from San Martín de las Pirámides, and most flights land in Teotihuacán municipality unless the weather turns.

Operators publish addresses, not field names. Globos Aerostáticos Teotihuacán gives Carretera México-Tulancingo km 28.2, San Martín de las Pirámides. Amigo Tours sends flight-only guests on {{< tour-link tour="696822" >}}its balloon flight with optional pyramids tour{{< /tour-link >}} to the Balloon's Paradise port in San Martín de las Pirámides (postcode 55850). The two {{< tour-link tour="1520860" >}}Vuelo en globo Teotihuacán flights{{< /tour-link >}} on GetYourGuide meet at Av. de la Amistad s/n, San Francisco Mazapa, Teotihuacán. Viaja Teotihuacan says its base is 5 minutes from the Pemex Pirámides gas station, Visita Teotihuacan says you will find it on Google as Gran Vuelo Teotihuacan, and We Fly puts its reception 5 minutes from the archaeological zone.

### Published balloon-port addresses from three operators

| Operator | Published address | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Volare | Carretera Libre a Tulancingo km 27.5, San Juan Teotihuacán | Search "Volare \| Vuelos en globo" in Google Maps or Waze, as Volare suggests, and compare it with your confirmation |
| We Fly | México–Tulancingo highway km 27.3, San Francisco Mazapa, in its how-to-get-there guide; km 28, San Martín de las Pirámides 55850, on its all-inclusive tour page | Ask for the pin for your booking, because the two pages give different kilometer markers |
| Sky Balloons México | Carretera México–Tulancingo km 28.0, int. 1, Francisco Villa, 55830 San Martín Centro | Confirm the entrance and that the reception has not moved |

The kilometer markers sit close together, but each is a different entrance with its own parking and reception desk. Do not type "Teotihuacán pyramids" or "hot air balloon Teotihuacán" into a map app as your final destination: those searches can send you to the archaeological zone, another operator or a viewpoint.

One trap catches self-drivers. We Fly's online FAQ prints the coordinates 19.6925, -98.8438 as its balloon port, but those are the coordinates of the centre of the archaeological site, the Pyramid of the Sun. Type them into a map app at 4:30 a.m. and you will end up at a closed monument. Use the pin your operator sends, not a set of coordinates copied from a website.

Each company has its own field and reception, so "the balloon port" means your operator's, not a shared terminal. If you have booked through Viator or GetYourGuide, the meeting point on your voucher is the one that counts.

### The archaeological zone is a separate destination

The pyramids have their own public entrances, run by INAH, Mexico's heritage institute. The zone opens from 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., with last entry at 4:30 p.m., so it is still closed when balloon check-in starts. If you plan to fly and then walk the site, give your driver two stops in this order:

1. Your operator's reception or launch field, early in the morning.
2. The entrance (gate) of the archaeological zone you want, after landing and any breakfast in your package.

Agree on a pickup point for after your visit as well. The zone has several gates, and "meet me by the pyramids" is not precise enough for a driver.

## How operators send you the location

For flight-only bookings, most operators confirm the place and time the day before. Viaja Teotihuacan, on its {{< tour-link tour="357659P1" >}}balloon flight with cave breakfast{{< /tour-link >}}, says it sends a message one day ahead with the exact Google Maps location and your arrival time. Skyteam, Teotihuacan Adventures, Visita Teotihuacan, Explora México and Vive Vuela say the same on their listings. Globos Aerostáticos Teotihuacán has a sales adviser confirm the time the day before and allows only 10 minutes' tolerance: arrive later and you lose the transport without a refund.

That message comes from the operator, not from Viator or GetYourGuide, so check that the phone number on your booking works in Mexico and keep data roaming on. If nothing has arrived by the afternoon before your flight, message the operator through the booking platform straight away.

Bring the ID you booked with. We Fly says it checks a valid government photo ID (a passport works) at reception to register you under the flight insurance, and without it you cannot board, though you can reschedule. Ask your operator whether it applies the same rule. We Fly also asks guests to arrive at least 30 minutes before flight time.

On dates we checked in late October 2026, flight-only options without transport ran from about $136 to $159 per person. The [prices guide](/teotihuacan-balloon-ride-prices/) breaks down what each package adds, and the [flight-only page](/teotihuacan-balloon-flight-only/) compares the options for people who make their own way.

## How far the balloon port is and when to leave

The route is the Mexico-Pachuca motorway (Mex 85D) north via Avenida Insurgentes Norte, then the Ecatepec-Pirámides toll road (132D). INAH lists that route for the archaeological zone, with the Mexico-Tuxpan road, the Arco Norte and the free Mexico-Teotihuacán road as alternatives. Your last few turns depend on your operator, so follow its pin and ignore the signs for the archaeological zone. Expect tolls; the 2026 tariff is unconfirmed, so carry pesos and a card.

We Fly measures 47.3 km from central Mexico City to Teotihuacán and gives 45 to 60 minutes by car, depending on traffic. Before dawn there is little traffic, so plan on roughly 45 to 60 minutes from Centro, Roma, Condesa or Polanco, and add time from the outer neighborhoods or for a transfer van that stops at several hotels. The drive back to the city later in the morning often takes longer.

It will be fully dark for the whole drive. Sunrise at Teotihuacán is around 6:33 in late October and 7:05 in late December, and as early as 5:57 in late June. Mexico City stays on UTC-6 all year, so there is no clock change to catch you out.

### What time to leave Mexico City

Work back from the check-in time your operator sends. Most fields expect passengers around 5:30 to 6:00 a.m. We Fly's own guide tells self-drivers to be at its field by 5:30 and to leave Mexico City by 4:30 a.m. If it is your first time on the route, leave earlier. Here is a sample plan for a 5:30 check-in from central Mexico City; your operator's time replaces it:

| Time | Step |
|---|---|
| 4:00 a.m. | Leave central Mexico City in a booked car (earlier from outer neighborhoods) |
| About 5:00 to 5:15 a.m. | Reach your operator's entrance, park and find the reception |
| 5:30 a.m. | Check in at the operator's confirmed point |
| After check-in | Coffee, the safety briefing and inflation; take-off depends on the weather |
| After landing | Follow the operator back to the reception, breakfast or the ruins |

Not every flight takes off at 6:30, and not every package returns at the same hour, so plan the rest of the day around your own confirmation.

## Option 1: Book a balloon tour with Mexico City pickup

For most visitors staying in Mexico City, the operator's transfer removes the problem. You meet a driver at your hotel or a central meeting point, and the van takes you to the right reception and brings you back on the package's schedule. Pickups usually run between about 4:00 and 5:30 a.m., depending on the operator and your neighborhood, and are confirmed the day before.

Two examples from the listings. Viaja Teotihuacan's {{< tour-link tour="357659P1" >}}balloon flight with cave breakfast, pyramids and Mexico City pickup{{< /tour-link >}}, rated 4.9 from more than 8,300 reviews on Viator, collects between about 4:20 and 5:10 a.m. depending on your location, from hotels or Airbnbs. The {{< tour-link tour="247495P2" >}}We Fly flight with breakfast at La Gruta and Mexico City pickup{{< /tour-link >}}, rated 4.9 from more than 4,800 reviews, starts pickups from 4:00 a.m. in central Mexico City and aims to reach Teotihuacán by about 5:40.

### Hotel pickup or a central meeting point

These are not always the same product. A listing may offer:

- Hotel pickup within named neighborhoods, sometimes only at hotels and never at rental apartments.
- A fixed meeting point, often at or near the Angel of Independence on Paseo de la Reforma.
- Transport sold as an add-on to a flight-only ticket.
- Round-trip transport as part of a longer package with breakfast, the archaeological zone or a shop stop before the return.

We Fly sells its own flight with Mexico City transfer for MXN 2,890 (about $158) per person, meeting near Reforma at 4:30 a.m.; it says the whole experience lasts up to 8.5 hours from pickup to return. Its all-inclusive flight, at MXN 3,600 (about $197), collects free from hotels in Reforma, Polanco, the Historic Center and Roma from 4:00 a.m. and has you back in the city by 3:00 p.m. Not every address qualifies elsewhere either: Globos Aerostáticos Teotihuacán does not collect from private homes or Airbnbs, and Arc Tours and Amigo Tours meet at the Angel itself. The [rides from Mexico City page](/teotihuacan-balloon-rides-from-mexico-city/) compares pickup zones and times.

Be on time. Volare's reservation page says guests get 5 minutes' tolerance before the driver moves on, and warns that its own van may run 5 to 15 minutes late in traffic. Be at the pickup point a few minutes early.

### What to ask before you pay

1. Is transport included in this price, or an extra?
2. Is pickup at my hotel or at a fixed meeting point? Ask for the written address and a map pin.
3. Is my exact address covered? Do not assume all of Roma, Condesa, Polanco, Reforma or the Centro qualifies.
4. When and how will I get the final pickup time: email, WhatsApp or the booking platform?
5. Who runs the van: the balloon operator, a separate driver or the platform's partner?
6. Where does the van drop me: my hotel, the Angel or somewhere else?
7. If the flight is cancelled for weather after I have been collected, what happens to the transfer and the refund?

A transfer suits first-time visitors, solo travelers who would rather not arrange a driver, families with children and anyone who wants a fixed ride back. The drawback is time: a shared van may stop at several hotels, a breakfast or a sightseeing stop, so confirm the return window before you book anything for the afternoon.

## Option 2: Book a private driver or taxi in advance

A private car makes sense with a flight-only ticket, a group of three or four, or a plan to spend longer at the ruins after landing. It also helps if you are staying outside the usual pickup areas.

Ask for a quote that covers the whole day and give the driver:

- The date and your pickup address.
- Your operator's exact launch-field address and map pin.
- The time you must arrive at the field.
- The number of passengers, bags and any child seats.
- Whether the driver waits, collects you after breakfast or comes back at a set time.
- Any stop at the archaeological zone, and which gate.
- The drop-off address, total fare, tolls, waiting charges and cancellation terms.

### Uber, DiDi and street taxis at 4 a.m.

Whether Uber, DiDi or a street taxi will take you from Mexico City to a launch field before dawn is unclear, and so are the cost and the chance of a car back from San Martín de las Pirámides. An app may show a price the evening before, but that does not mean a driver will accept at 4 a.m. Do not leave it to the morning. Arrange it the evening before: book a hotel taxi or a private driver, agree the pickup time and the address from your operator's pin, and settle how you will get back.

The return is the harder half. Many flight-only guests head to the ruins after landing, and the zone opens at 8:00, so ask your operator whether it can drop you at a gate or call a local taxi. A delayed or cancelled flight also changes when you need the driver, and waiting charges can cancel out the saving on a flight-only ticket.

## Option 3: Drive yourself to the balloon port

Driving works for a group that already has a rental car, and it gives you the most freedom after the flight. Our guide to [booking the flight without a tour](/guides/teotihuacan-balloon-ride-without-a-tour-flight-only/) compares the self-arrival tickets that go with it.

| Visita Teotihuacan | Offices in Teotihuacán (Gran Vuelo Teotihuacan) | Day before |
| Amigo Tours (Balloon's Paradise) | San Martín de las Pirámides 55850 | On the listing |
| Volare | Volare balloon port, km 27.5 | Listed meeting point |

| We Fly | Reception 5 min from the zone | Listed meeting point |

Parking is on site at the operators that mention it: We Fly offers free parking and Cielo Azteca a secure lot at its take-off area. The {{< tour-link tour="171986P1" >}}Volare flight without transport{{< /tour-link >}} counts 3 hours from arrival at its port to departure. [Our Volare review](/tours/volare-teotihuacan-balloon-flight-from-mexico-city/) covers the rest.

### Before you drive

- Put the operator's reception pin into your map app the evening before and check it against the written address.
- Download the area for offline use in case mobile data drops.
- Ask whether parking is free and whether you need to register the car.
- Allow time for toll booths, slow local turns and finding the reception in the dark.
- Do not drive straight back to the city on very little sleep; share the driving if you can.
- Take a phone charger, your ID, water and a warm layer; it is cold in the valley before sunrise.

### Driving from the launch field to the pyramids

You can, once the flight is over. Balloons land wherever the wind takes them, often away from the field, and the ground crew drives you back. Ask the crew when you will be back at the reception, and do not leave until the operator has finished its return run. The zone opens at 8:00 a.m. and has its own parking. INAH charges MXN 210 (about $11) for international visitors and MXN 105 (about $6) for Mexican citizens and residents with ID; admission is not part of a balloon ticket unless your package says so.

## Option 4: Take the public bus, but not to a sunrise check-in

Autobuses Teotihuacán runs from Terminal Central del Norte, but its ticket window at platform 7 opens at 6:00 a.m. and takes cash only. By then you should already be at the field. The buses also stop at gates 1 and 2 of the archaeological zone, not at the launch fields in San Martín de las Pirámides. The Metro Potrero and Potrero-Apan bus route has the same problem.

### Taking the bus for a daytime visit to the ruins

The bus is a good way to visit the ruins later in the day:

1. Take Metro Line 5 to Autobuses del Norte, next to the Terminal Central del Norte.
2. Find the Autobuses Teotihuacán desk. The bus company gives platform 7; Mexico City's tourism guide describes the "Pirámides" ticket sellers by Puerta 8 at the north end of the building. Follow the signs or ask staff.
3. Check that your bus stops at the archaeological zone itself; some only reach the town of San Juan Teotihuacán. The company puts the ride at about 60 minutes in normal traffic.
4. Return buses leave from gate 2 every 15 minutes, according to the company. Ask about the last departure on the day.

Fares change, so check the current price at the desk. The bus does not include admission to the zone.

### A bus back after the balloon flight

If your flight includes a ride back to the operator's field but not to Mexico City, you can take the bus from the zone later in the day. Do not assume the field is within walking distance of gate 2: you may need a local taxi or a lift from the operator between the two. Arrange it before the morning.

## Is flight-only plus your own transport cheaper?

Sometimes. A flight-only fare is only half the comparison if you also pay to reach a field in the countryside and get back.

We Fly shows the gap on its own site: its traditional shared flight, where you make your own way to the field by 5:30 a.m. and get a buffet breakfast, costs MXN 2,300 (about $126), and the flight with Mexico City transfer costs MXN 2,890 (about $158). That is MXN 590 (about $32) per person, or about $64 for two, though the packages may differ in more than the ride. A private driver for the morning can cost more than that; a group of four sharing one booked car can come out ahead.

Add it up the same way for your own booking: flight tickets, the ride there, the ride back, tolls or parking, any waiting charge and any extras you want. Compare that total with a transfer package for the same number of people and the same inclusions (breakfast, zone entry). A bare sunrise flight against a full-day combo is not a transport comparison. Our [cheap balloon rides guide](/guides/cheap-teotihuacan-balloon-rides/) and [prices page](/teotihuacan-balloon-ride-prices/) run the numbers.

## Pickup from Roma, Condesa, Reforma, Polanco or Centro Histórico

These central neighborhoods appear on most pickup lists, but coverage depends on the operator and the package. A hotel on Reforma may get a door pickup while an apartment a few streets away has to meet the van at the Angel. "Mexico City hotel pickup" on a listing does not mean every address in the city.

- Roma or Condesa: compare a hotel pickup with a meeting point on Reforma, and count the separate ride you need to reach that point at 4 a.m.
- Polanco: check that your package collects in Polanco; some all-inclusive flights do, while cheaper ones use a meeting point near Reforma.
- Centro Histórico: give the exact address and ask whether the driver can stop outside your building.
- Near Reforma: a meeting point by the Angel can be quicker than a van that stops at several hotels; use the spot in your confirmation.
- Outside the central areas: ask for a custom pickup quote or book a private driver.

A good confirmation gives the pickup spot's name, the street address or pin, the time window, a contact number, how to recognize the vehicle and where you will be dropped off.

## Staying in San Juan Teotihuacán instead

The other route is to sleep near the field. A night near San Martín de las Pirámides or San Juan Teotihuacán removes the 4 a.m. departure from Mexico City and makes a flight-only ticket easier to use, which suits photographers, families with young children and anyone spending the afternoon at the ruins. We Fly sells a Teotihuacán hotel night with a 15-minute transfer to the field. Our [where to stay page](/where-to-stay-teotihuacan-balloon-ride/) lists hotels near the launch area.

Staying close does not mean you can walk to the field. Check the road distance from your hotel to the operator's reception, and ask the hotel to book a taxi for the morning, because a 5:30 check-in leaves no room to find one on the spot. If you are visiting the zone afterwards, ask whether you can leave your bags or car at the hotel and how you will get to the gate.

## Five common transport mistakes

### 1. Driving to the pyramid gates instead of the balloon field

The zone and the balloon reception are different addresses. Ask for the operator's Google Maps pin and check the name on it.

### 2. Reading "transport available" as "transport included"

Look for round-trip Mexico City transfer in your ticket's inclusions. Many operators sell transport as an add-on to the advertised price.

### 3. Planning on the 6:00 a.m. bus for a 5:30 check-in

It cannot work. Book a transfer, a driver, or a night near the field with a taxi arranged for the morning.

### 4. Booking only the ride there

The way back is harder to find. Plan how you will get back from the field, the breakfast restaurant or the zone gate, depending on what you booked.

### 5. Booking something else straight after

Weather delays, a second flight wave, extra hotel stops and Mexico City traffic all move the finish time. Leave a gap; an advertised return time is not a promise.

## A booking checklist to save on your phone

Send these to your operator or driver:

- The operator's name, the reception address and its map pin
- The flight date, check-in time and passenger names
- With a transfer: the pickup point, time window and the driver's contact
- If you drive: parking instructions and how to find the right entrance
- With a private driver: the waiting policy, return time and total price
- If you are visiting the ruins: which gate, whether admission is included, and how long you have there
- What happens if the weather cancels the flight
- A way to reach the operator if your car is running late

Or send this message:

> Hello, I have booked a balloon flight for [date] under [name]. Could you please send me the exact Google Maps pin for check-in, the arrival time, and the return transport details? Could you also confirm whether the archaeological zone and the transfer are included in my ticket? Thank you!

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is the Teotihuacán balloon port?

Each operator has its own launch field and reception in the valley around the archaeological zone, mostly in San Martín de las Pirámides, many along the México–Tulancingo road. Use the location your operator sends the day before, not the pyramid coordinates printed on some websites.

### Is the balloon launch site inside the archaeological zone?

No. Check-in is at the operator's field or reception. The zone has its own entrances, opening hours (8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m.) and admission.

### How early is pickup from Mexico City for a sunrise balloon ride?

Usually between about 4:00 and 5:30 a.m., depending on the operator and your neighborhood. The operator confirms the time and place the day before; some collect from hotels, others use a meeting point near Reforma.

### Can I take a bus to my sunrise balloon flight?

No. The Terminal Norte ticket window opens at 6:00 a.m., after most check-ins, and the buses stop only at the zone gates. The bus works well for a daytime visit to the ruins.

### Can I take an Uber to the balloon ride?

Ride-hailing at 4 a.m. is uncertain, and so is finding a car for the return. Book a hotel taxi or private driver the evening before, or choose a package with transport.

### How long is the drive from Mexico City?

Roughly 45 to 60 minutes before dawn from the central neighborhoods, on the Mexico-Pachuca motorway and the Ecatepec-Pirámides toll road. Add time for parking and finding the reception.

### Is there parking at the balloon port?

At the operators that mention it, yes: We Fly offers free parking and Cielo Azteca a secure lot. Ask yours when it sends the location.

### Do packages pick up in Roma or Condesa?

Some do, within each operator's pickup area. Give your full address before you pay and get the pickup confirmed in writing.

### Can I visit the pyramids after the balloon ride?

Yes, if your package or driver leaves enough time. The zone opens at 8:00 a.m., last entry is 4:30 p.m., and admission is extra unless your package includes it. Our [pyramids packages guide](/guides/teotihuacan-balloon-ride-pyramids-tour-packages/) compares the combined tours.

### Can I get back to Mexico City by bus after the ruins?

Yes, during the day. Autobuses Teotihuacán boards return buses at gate 2 about every 15 minutes. Check the last departure, and how you will reach gate 2 from where your morning ends.

### What if the flight is cancelled because of the weather?

Follow your operator's policy, and ask before booking who pays for a driver who has already collected you or waited at the field; transport and booking-platform terms can differ from the operator's. Our [weather and cancellations guide](/guides/teotihuacan-balloon-weather-cancellations/) explains what usually happens.

## Which option suits you

- For the simplest sunrise: a flight with round-trip transfer, with the pickup point, drop-off and weather terms confirmed in writing.
- For the most freedom: a flight-only ticket and a booked private driver, especially if you want several hours at the pyramids.
- For a group with a rental car: drive yourself, with the operator's pin saved and parking confirmed.
- For the cheapest visit to the ruins alone: the daytime bus from Terminal Norte, which cannot get you to a balloon.

Whichever you choose, get the exact check-in location and time from your operator first, then book the ride. Planning the rest of the morning?
