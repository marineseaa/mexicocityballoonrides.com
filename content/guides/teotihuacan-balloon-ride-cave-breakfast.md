La Gruta and La Cueva are two different restaurants, a short drive apart, and "breakfast in a cave" on a balloon listing can mean either, or a third venue. If La Gruta is the one you want, book a package that names it: WE FLY's Photos + La Gruta costs MXN 2,990 (about $164) without transport, and its All-Inclusive costs MXN 3,600 (about $197) with Mexico City pickup and a guided pyramids visit. WE FLY moved its package breakfasts from La Cueva to La Gruta on 22 August 2026, so older reviews that mention La Cueva describe the previous arrangement. Many other operators still sell an unnamed "natural cave", so get the restaurant's name in writing before you pay.

## La Gruta and La Cueva side by side

La Gruta publishes its hours, takes reservations from anyone and is named on several balloon packages. La Cueva suits you if your operator includes it, but its breakfast hours are not published consistently. Decide first whether you want a particular restaurant or just a meal after the flight.

| What matters | La Gruta | La Cueva |
|---|---|---|
| Same place? | No | No |
| Location | San Juan Teotihuacán, about 200 m (650 ft) from Gate 5, behind the Pyramid of the Sun | Cerrada Hombres Ilustres, San Francisco Mazapa, outside the archaeological zone |
| Setting | Large volcanic grotto with a long-running restaurant | A separate cave restaurant with its own dining room |

| Booking on your own | Reservations through its website; advised at weekends | Contact the restaurant directly (594 688 1261) |

### La Gruta, the cave restaurant by the pyramids

La Gruta is a long-established restaurant inside a natural volcanic cavity, about 200 m from Gate 5 and behind the Pyramid of the Sun. Its menu draws on regional ingredients, and it has a children's menu, a children's play area and free parking for diners.

Much of the appeal is the setting. After an hour in an open basket, walking down into a broad stone-walled grotto feels like a second outing. That change of scene is what you pay extra for on a premium package; nobody can promise the food is better than at a buffet.

La Gruta's website gives its hours as 8:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. every day of the year and advises booking at weekends. It holds a table for 10 minutes; after that, the reservation is no longer guaranteed, and a limited number of slots goes out for each time. That matters after a balloon flight, because wind and the recovery drive can make you late. If you book it yourself, say you are coming from a balloon flight and ask what happens if you are delayed.

A table you book yourself is not the same as a tour meal voucher. On your own, you order from the menu and pay the restaurant. On a tour, you usually get one main course and set drinks, and anything else costs extra.

### La Cueva, a different restaurant from La Gruta

La Cueva's own website gives its address as Cerrada Hombres Ilustres, San Francisco Mazapa; WE FLY places it outside the archaeological zone, between Gates 4 and 5. It is not a second entrance to La Gruta: a booking at one will not be honoured at the other.

Its hours are uncertain. WE FLY's restaurant guide lists 8:30 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. daily but says it could not confirm the opening time, and the Visite Teotihuacan listing gives noon to 6:00 p.m., which reads as a lunch service. La Cueva's own site does not publish hours. Do not count on an 8:30 a.m. breakfast there unless the restaurant or your operator confirms it in writing.

Watch for old package descriptions too. WE FLY says La Cueva was its previous breakfast partner and that, from 22 August 2026, its package guests eat at La Gruta. That covers WE FLY's own products only: other companies may still use La Cueva. {{< tour-link tour="484176P1" >}}Arc Tours' flight package{{< /tour-link >}}, for example, offers its buffet "optional in the Restaurant La Cueva" (4.99/5, but from only 72 reviews).

If a listing still mentions La Cueva, ask the provider: "Is breakfast on my date at La Cueva, La Gruta or another restaurant? Can you confirm the name and address?"

### Gran Vuelo Traditional, the lowest-priced cave breakfast here

At MXN 2,599, Gran Vuelo's Traditional Flight includes a shared flight of 35 to 50 minutes, breakfast in a natural cave, welcome coffee, a flight certificate, a toast and a tasting of traditional drinks. Round-trip transport from Mexico City and the entry ticket are add-ons, and the product page does not name the restaurant.

It suits you if you have a car or are staying near Teotihuacán. If you want La Gruta specifically, ask Gran Vuelo which cave it uses.

### WE FLY Photos + La Gruta, the named restaurant with photos

WE FLY's Photos + La Gruta costs MXN 2,990 per person and combines a shared flight, professional photos and a set breakfast at La Gruta. WE FLY's restaurant guide describes that breakfast as fruit with amaranth and honey, two drinks and one main course from enchiladas, chilaquiles, quesadillas or barbacoa flautas, valued at MXN 600; you do not get the full menu.

It suits you if the flight and the restaurant matter most and you will get to Teotihuacán on your own. If you want pickup in Mexico City, add it or pick a product that includes it.

### Magic Balloons Premium and Gran Vuelo Explorer, with transport and entry

Magic Balloons' Premium costs MXN 3,200 per person, with a shared flight, round-trip Mexico City transport, entry to the archaeological zone and a breakfast in a cave. The description names neither La Gruta nor La Cueva and does not list a guide, so confirm both if they matter to you.

Gran Vuelo's Explorer Flight costs MXN 3,299 and adds shared round-trip transport and entry to its cave breakfast flight. Its itinerary calls the visit to the zone self-guided, with a guide available as an extra. Pick it if you want one booking for the morning and do not need a guide at the ruins.

### WE FLY All-Inclusive, La Gruta with a guide at the ruins

WE FLY's All-Inclusive costs MXN 3,600 and includes hotel pickup in central Mexico City, a 45- to 60-minute flight, breakfast at La Gruta, entry to the zone and a visit with a certified bilingual guide. Its sample itinerary starts with pickup around 4:00 to 4:30 a.m. and gets you back to the city between about 11:30 a.m. and 1:00 p.m. Your confirmation sets the real pickup point and time.

WE FLY calls the breakfast "exclusive", but La Gruta is not a private WE FLY restaurant: it serves ordinary diners and takes reservations from anyone.

Gran Vuelo's Archaeological Flight, at MXN 3,599, sits next to it on price, but its product page lists a certified guided tour while its timeline says self-guided visit. Get that settled in writing before you compare the two.

## Does a cave breakfast package save money?

Sometimes, though the bigger saving is often in effort. Compare the cost of the whole morning, not just the gap between two headline prices.

These sums use the operators' published prices:

| Comparison | Difference | What the extra buys |
|---|---:|---|
| WE FLY shared flight MXN 2,300 vs Photos + La Gruta MXN 2,990 | MXN 690 (about $38) per person | Photos and the set breakfast at La Gruta |
| Magic Balloons Experience MXN 2,899 vs Premium MXN 3,200 | MXN 301 (about $16) per person | A cave breakfast in place of the buffet, on otherwise similar packages |
| Gran Vuelo Traditional MXN 2,599 vs Explorer MXN 3,299 | MXN 700 (about $38) per person | Shared transport from Mexico City and the entry ticket, on top of the cave breakfast |

For a couple, the MXN 301 upgrade is MXN 602 and the MXN 700 gap is MXN 1,400. Whether that is good value depends on what you would otherwise pay for the transport, the entry, the meal and the photos.

Don't count the food twice. If the cheaper package already includes a buffet, the dearer one upgrades the place and the menu; it does not add a second meal. Ask whether tips, special drinks and extra dishes are covered.

## What you eat at La Gruta after the flight

On your own, you order from a breakfast menu of chilaquiles, eggs your way, quesadillas, enchiladas, barbacoa flautas and café de olla, with a wider menu later in the day. WE FLY's guide, using menu prices published in September 2026, lists chilaquiles at MXN 221, a fruit plate at MXN 149 and café de olla at MXN 84: MXN 454 (about $25) before extras and tip. Menus and prices change, so check La Gruta's [own menu](https://www.lagruta.mx/menu/) for your date.

Breakfast is a different meal from the mole and lamb barbacoa La Gruta is known for later in the day, and a set tour breakfast will not include them.

### Is the La Gruta tour breakfast a buffet?

No. WE FLY describes its La Gruta breakfast as a set menu of fruit, two drinks and a choice of main course. The buffet on its other packages is served at a different restaurant, La Martina Teotihuacán, behind the Pyramid of the Moon.

Before you book, ask the operator:

1. Is breakfast à la carte, a set menu or a buffet?
2. Which main dishes and drinks are included?
3. Can they cater for vegetarian or other diets?
4. Is there a charge for anything off the package menu?
5. Are service and tips included?
6. Does the price include a confirmed table after the flight?

### Does La Gruta have vegetarian and family choices?

La Gruta's menu has a vegetarian mixiote with wild mushrooms, nopal and potato, a children's menu, and the restaurant has a play area. That helps if you book on your own, but a tour's set breakfast may not offer a substitute, so ask in advance about allergies and diets.

## What breakfast at La Cueva costs

La Cueva does not publish a breakfast menu with prices. The Visite Teotihuacan listing puts a meal at about MXN 500 (about $27) per person, and WE FLY's guide quotes MXN 450 (about $25) for a breakfast without a package. Treat both as estimates, not a current price.

If La Cueva is your choice, ask the restaurant for:

- Its earliest confirmed breakfast seating on your date.
- A current menu, or the set meal attached to your balloon tour.
- The final price per adult and child, with taxes and any deposit.
- Whether early breakfast is open to independent diners or only to groups.
- The address and how you get there from the landing or the meeting point.

Do not book a La Cueva package because an older blog post says WE FLY takes all its guests there. WE FLY's current packages use La Gruta.

## Eating at La Gruta without a balloon ride

Yes, you can. La Gruta is an ordinary public restaurant, and its website has reservations, hours and the menu.

You can also turn it round: book a flight-only ticket, get to the restaurant afterwards and pay for breakfast yourself. You choose the meal and how long you spend at the pyramids, and it works when no package with La Gruta suits your dates.

At weekends, book ahead, especially for an early table. A balloon company's landing time is an estimate, so ask the restaurant how long it holds a table and allow for the drive from the landing.

## Planning the flight, the cave breakfast and the pyramids

This timetable is an example for planning, not any operator's schedule. Weather moves the start, the inflation and the landing. Breakfast in the cave comes after the flight, and the archaeological zone after breakfast if you add it.

| Approximate time | What happens | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| 4:30–5:30 a.m. (some at 4:00) | Leave Mexico City, if transport is included | Some packages collect from hotels, others from a central meeting point |
| 5:30–6:15 a.m. | Check-in and briefing at the balloon port | Confirm the launch-site address the day before |
| Around sunrise | Inflation, take-off and the flight | The pilot and the weather set the start and the length |
| After landing | Drive back with the crew, photos, toast if included | Recovery can take longer than planned |
| Around 8:00–9:30 a.m. | Breakfast at the confirmed restaurant | La Gruta opens at 8:00 a.m.; other venues differ |
| After breakfast | Optional visit to the archaeological zone | Check the entry ticket and the guide separately |
| Late morning to early afternoon | Back to Mexico City | Depends on the stops, the time at the ruins and traffic |

INAH lists the archaeological zone as open 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., with last entry at 4:30 p.m. and admission of MXN 210 for international visitors; discounts and free entry apply to some visitors. A restaurant breakfast or a balloon flight does not include that ticket.

If the ruins are a priority, check how long your package gives you there. A 45-minute stop with a coordinator is very different from a visit of several hours. Our [balloon and pyramids packages comparison](/guides/teotihuacan-balloon-ride-pyramids-tour-packages/) explains those differences.

### Is there time to eat at La Gruta and see the pyramids?

Usually yes, but how much you see depends on your return time. La Gruta is close to Gate 5, which makes the two easy to combine, though there is no door from the dining room into the site. Ask your operator where you will be dropped and which gate you will use.

With your own transport you can stay at the zone after breakfast as long as you like. On a shared transfer, you leave when the group leaves.

### What if wind cancels the flight?

If the pilot judges the wind unsafe, the flight does not go. What happens next depends on the operator's written terms. Some offer a new date, and breakfast, transport and entry tickets can be handled differently from the flight itself.

Ask before you pay:

- Can we still go to the restaurant if the flight is cancelled?
- Does the package qualify for a full refund or only part of it?
- If the flight moves to another day, does the La Gruta or La Cueva table move too?
- If I booked the restaurant myself, who tells it?

Our [booking and cancellation guide](/guides/how-to-book-teotihuacan-balloon-ride/) has the full checklist.

## What to check before booking a cave breakfast package

"Breakfast in a cave" sounds precise but leaves out the details that matter for value. Go through this list on the booking page and ask for written answers to anything missing:

- **Restaurant:** La Gruta, La Cueva or another venue? Ask for the address.
- **Meal:** set menu, buffet or à la carte? Which dishes and drinks?
- **Transport:** round trip from Mexico City, one way or none? Hotel pickup or a meeting point?
- **Flight:** shared or private basket? Roughly how long in the air?
- **Pyramids:** entry included? How long at the site? A guide inside?
- **Photos:** included, sold by the crew afterwards or not offered?
- **Age, weight and health:** any limits or surcharges from the flight operator?
- **Weather:** what happens to the flight, breakfast, transport and entry if the flight is cancelled?
- **Timing:** pickup, breakfast table and return to Mexico City.
- **Final price:** taxes, transport supplements, weekend rates, extras and tips.

A clearly described package that costs a little more is a better buy than a cheaper one that turns out to leave out the ride back or the restaurant you wanted.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are La Gruta and La Cueva the same restaurant?

No. They are two restaurants near Teotihuacán with different addresses and bookings. La Gruta is near Gate 5, behind the Pyramid of the Sun; La Cueva is in San Francisco Mazapa.

### Which Teotihuacán balloon tours include La Gruta?

WE FLY's Photos + La Gruta at MXN 2,990 and All-Inclusive at MXN 3,600 both name La Gruta, and on the marketplace WE FLY's {{< tour-link tour="247495P2" >}}flight with La Gruta breakfast{{< /tour-link >}} does too. Paseos Olmedo's {{< tour-link tour="193670P3" >}}flight with a stop at La Gruta{{< /tour-link >}} (about $350, 4.9/5 from 150+ reviews) takes you there but does not pay for the food. Other operators' restaurants can change by date, so do not read a named restaurant into "cave breakfast".

### Does WE FLY still serve breakfast at La Cueva?

No. WE FLY says breakfast on its packages moved from La Cueva to La Gruta on 22 August 2026. Other companies make their own arrangements.

### Is breakfast at La Gruta included with every balloon ride?

No. Many flight-only and standard tickets include a different breakfast, a snack or no proper meal. You need a package that names breakfast at La Gruta.

### What time does La Gruta open?

8:00 a.m., every day of the year, and it closes at 6:00 p.m., according to its website. It recommends booking at weekends. Check the hours for your date.

### Does La Cueva open early enough for breakfast after a sunrise flight?

That is not confirmed. One listing gives 8:30 a.m. and another noon, and La Cueva's own site does not say. Ask La Cueva or your operator for a confirmed breakfast time before you book.

### How much is a balloon ride with cave breakfast in Teotihuacán?

On the operators' own sites, Gran Vuelo's Traditional Flight with an unnamed natural-cave breakfast costs MXN 2,599 (about $142), WE FLY's Photos + La Gruta costs MXN 2,990 (about $164), and packages that add transport and the ruins cost more. Prices change.

### Do I need a pyramids ticket to eat at La Gruta?

No. La Gruta takes ordinary restaurant bookings, and a meal there does not include the archaeological-zone ticket. Ask the restaurant for the best way in from Gate 5.

### Can I book La Gruta myself after a flight-only balloon ride?

Yes. Book a table with the restaurant, arrange the ride from your balloon operator's meeting point and pay for the meal yourself. Leave slack, because launch and landing times depend on the weather.

### Is a cave breakfast better than a buffet?

It depends on what you want from the morning. The cave gives you the setting; a buffet is simpler and often cheaper. Some travellers pay gladly for the atmosphere, others would rather have the extra time at the ruins. Ask which menu comes with each package; the cave meal is not unlimited.

### Does a cave breakfast package include transport from Mexico City?

Not always. Gran Vuelo's Traditional Flight and WE FLY's Photos + La Gruta do not include it, while WE FLY's All-Inclusive, Gran Vuelo's Explorer Flight and Magic Balloons' Premium do.

### Can children fly and eat at La Gruta?

La Gruta has a children's menu and a play area, but each balloon operator sets its own age and height limits. Check those, plus the child prices for the restaurant and the archaeological zone.

### Can you see the pyramids from the balloon?

Flights over the Teotihuacán valley can give you fine views of the pyramids, but nobody can guarantee a path directly over a monument, because the wind sets the route. A breakfast package does not change that. Our [sunrise balloon ride guide](/guides/teotihuacan-sunrise-hot-air-balloon-ride/) covers timing and what to expect.

## La Gruta or La Cueva with your balloon ride

Choose La Gruta if a well-documented cave restaurant is part of why you are going. It publishes its location, hours and reservations, WE FLY's current packages name it, and you can eat there without any balloon package.

Choose La Cueva if you have your own confirmed reservation or your operator clearly includes it on your date. Its hours conflict between listings and some older tour descriptions are out of date, so get an early breakfast time and the address in your booking.

Choose an unnamed "cave breakfast" package only if you are happy for the operator to pick the venue, or once you have the restaurant's name in writing. For the lowest total, an ordinary breakfast or a flight-only ticket may make more sense, and if the ruins come first, choose on the entry ticket, the guide and the time at the site.

For a first visit from Mexico City, the easiest morning is a package with round-trip transport, a sunrise flight, a named breakfast restaurant and enough time at the ruins. Read the full inclusions before you book, because the photo of a cave dining room tells you nothing about what the ticket covers.
