## The flights are not over the city

No balloon on this site takes off in Mexico City or flies over its skyline. The flights operate over the Teotihuacán valley in the State of Mexico, roughly 45 to 50 km northeast of the city by road. Most balloons launch around San Martín de las Pirámides, beside the Teotihuacán archaeological zone; a state official put the share of take-offs there at about 90% in February 2026. The drive takes roughly 45 to 60 minutes, and our page on [balloon rides from Mexico City](/teotihuacan-balloon-rides-from-mexico-city/) compares the transfers.

## Pickup and the pre-dawn start

Hotel pickups from Mexico City typically leave between about 4:30 and 5:40 a.m., and operators confirm the exact time the day before. We Fly asks guests to be at its reception for check-in and coffee around 5:30, with photo ID. We Fly says its free pickup covers hotels within 4 km of the Angel of Independence, in Reforma, Juárez, Zona Rosa, the Centro Histórico, Roma and Condesa, and quotes farther areas on request; Polanco is not on that list. Globos Aerostáticos Teotihuacán says its transport does not collect from private homes or Airbnbs.

Public transport will not get you there in time. The Autobuses Teotihuacán ticket window at Terminal Norte opens at 6:00, and the buses stop at the archaeological zone's gates, not at the launch fields.

## When to go

Balloons fly all year, but the dry season from November to April is steadier. Historical averages from the national weather service's San Martín de las Pirámides station show about 31 mm of rain a month or less in those months, against 106 to 120 mm from June to August. Dry-season dawns are cold: the average minimum is about 2 to 4 °C from December to March, and frost is possible in midwinter. Winter cold fronts bring gusts: on 1 February 2026, every flight in the valley was cancelled for wind. In the rainy season, showers in the Valley of Mexico usually arrive in the afternoon or evening, so many summer mornings still fly. The [best time for a Teotihuacán balloon ride](/guides/best-time-teotihuacan-balloon-ride/) goes month by month.

## What you actually see

The wind decides the route. Operators say they look for currents that carry the balloons toward the archaeological zone, but none promises it, and Globos Aerostáticos Teotihuacán states that it does not guarantee any direction or route. On a good morning you see the Pyramid of the Sun, the Pyramid of the Moon and the Avenue of the Dead from above; on others you watch them from one side of the valley. Read [do balloons fly over the Teotihuacán pyramids?](/guides/do-balloons-fly-over-teotihuacan-pyramids/) before you count on a particular shot.

## When the flight is cancelled

The pilot decides each morning whether to launch, and balloons fly only in light winds; fog and prolonged rain also ground them. Cancelling on your side is simpler: Viator's standard policy, which applies to all four flights in our comparison table, is a full refund if you cancel at least 24 hours before the start time. A weather cancellation is handled on the operator's terms. We Fly reschedules you to another sunrise at no cost; Globos Aerostáticos Teotihuacán reschedules the flight but does not refund extras such as transport or breakfast. Book a morning early in your trip. The guide to [weather cancellations and refunds](/guides/teotihuacan-balloon-weather-cancellations/) lists the policies we found.

## Safety checks

Star ratings tell you how a morning went, not whether the paperwork is in order. The federal aviation agency, AFAC, requires an operating permit, licensed pilots and passenger insurance, and the authorities advise travelers to confirm the permit before boarding. On 29 March 2026, AFAC inspected 23 of the 46 balloons operating in the valley and suspended three establishments for irregularities it did not specify. The worst accident on record here was the fire on 1 April 2023 that killed two passengers.
