For a balloon flight and a properly guided visit in one booking, take the top option of the {{< tour-link tour="696822" >}}Amigo Tours balloon flight with optional pyramids tour{{< /tour-link >}} on GetYourGuide. It adds a professional guide, the Teotihuacán entry ticket and the Basilica of Guadalupe on the way back. The listing starts at $148 for its cheapest option; the same product on Viator, the {{< tour-link tour="5885P459" >}}Teotihuacan and Guadalupe Shrine full-day tour{{< /tour-link >}}, priced the guided option at $249 on dates we checked in late October 2026. It is rated 4.6 from 1,321 reviews on GetYourGuide. If you only want time to wander, cheaper packages give you that. [Our review →](/tours/amigo-tours-teotihuacan-balloon-flight-optional-pyramids/)

## Free time is not a guided tour

Most balloon packages that mention the pyramids give you time on foot, not a guide. {{< tour-link tour="171986P1" >}}Volare's option with extra time at the ruins{{< /tour-link >}} costs the same $186 as its plain transport option, and the listing says plainly that the entrance fee and a guide are not included. {{< tour-link tour="357659P1" >}}Viaja Teotihuacan's $236 package{{< /tour-link >}} lists the archaeological-zone ticket and a certified guide among the exclusions. {{< tour-link tour="366381P1" >}}Tlatoani's all-inclusive option{{< /tour-link >}}, about $240, gives up to two hours. {{< tour-link tour="482323P1" >}}Skyteam's listing{{< /tour-link >}} notes that a guide can be hired on site.

That suits you if you have read up or want to set your own pace. If you want the story of the city told as you walk, book a package that names a guide.

- {{< tour-link tour="247495P2" >}}We Fly's all-included option{{< /tour-link >}}, about $231, lists a certified guide in the archaeological area with 1 to 1.5 hours on foot. [Our review →](/tours/we-fly-teotihuacan-balloon-breakfast-la-gruta/)

## Is the entry ticket included?

Usually not. Foreign visitors paid MX$210 in 2026, and Mexican nationals and foreign residents MX$105. Under-13s and over-60s enter free, by INAH's rules. Free Sundays apply only to Mexicans and foreign residents with ID.

The packages above with a guide all include the ticket. We Fly's separate {{< tour-link tour="247495P3" >}}all-inclusive option{{< /tour-link >}}, about $258, includes the access ticket and three hours of free time but no guide inside the zone. {{< tour-link tour="377048P1" >}}Globo Tetris{{< /tour-link >}} and {{< tour-link tour="463468P2" >}}Aventuras Teotihuacan{{< /tour-link >}} also sell all-inclusive options with entry. Everywhere else, carry pesos for the gate: Volare's listing says the fee is paid in cash.

## What you can and cannot climb

You cannot climb the Pyramid of the Sun. INAH's March 2026 bulletin still prohibits it, and it has been closed since 2020. The Pyramid of the Moon reopened its first tier in May 2025, but INAH closed access again after the April 2026 shooting "until further notice", and we know of no reopening since, so check before you go. Some listings still promise a climb; plan to see both pyramids from the Avenue of the Dead. Whether the balloon passes over them depends on the wind, as our guide on [flying over the pyramids](/guides/do-balloons-fly-over-teotihuacan-pyramids/) explains.

## The Guadalupe combo

Of the packages on this page, the Amigo Tours guided option alone adds the Basilica of Guadalupe, which sits in the north of Mexico City near the road back from Teotihuacán. The coach drops everyone at the starting point, the Ángel de la Independencia, but you can stay at the Basilica and make your own way back. On Viator the twin listing offers a balloon-only option with a 15-minute flight at $157 against $172 for about 45 minutes, so check which flight you are booking.

## How the day runs, and what to bring

Balloons lift off around first light, and the archaeological zone opens at 8:00, so a ruins visit slots in after landing and breakfast. Last entry is at 4:30 p.m. Expect to be back in Mexico City in the early afternoon: Excellentravel returns you at about 1:00 p.m., and one operator's terms give about 2:30 p.m. when the ruins are added. The Guadalupe stop makes the Amigo Tours day longer still.

INAH recommends water, a hat and sunscreen for the site. Drones are not allowed in the zone, and bag checks at the entrances were reported after the April 2026 shooting, so carry a small bag only. Amigo Tours' listing also bars backpacks and large bags, and asks you to bring a passport or ID card. The walk along the Avenue of the Dead is long, a couple of kilometers end to end, so wear the closed shoes you flew in.

## Who each package suits

Amigo Tours suits first-time visitors who want the whole day planned. Excellentravel and Luxury Tours suit couples or families who want their own guide and car. Volare's ruins option suits independent walkers on a budget.
